# Interview Guide

Use this mode before interviews happen, or after an analysis to plan the next round. The quality of the evidence is set in the room: leading questions produce answers the analysis then has to discount.

## Produce

1. **Objective:** what decision the interviews should inform, and which assumptions they test.
2. **Who to interview:** the personas, with a note on who would add missing perspectives (people who left or declined, prospects, the other side of a two-sided relationship).
3. **Question guide:** 8 to 12 behavioral questions grouped by topic, each with one or two follow-up probes. Ask about specific past events, not opinions or hypotheticals.
4. **Interviewer briefing:** the do's and don'ts below, in a short checklist the interviewer can keep in front of them.
5. **For filmed interviews:** a short list of moments to capture for the planned content (for example, an origin story, a before-and-after, a specific case), still asked behaviorally.

## Good question patterns

- "Tell me about the last time…"
- "Walk me through exactly what happened."
- "What did you do next?"
- "How often does that happen? What did it cost you?"
- "What have you tried instead?"
- "What almost stopped you from…?"

## Rewrite leading questions

| Leading or presupposing | Behavioral alternative |
|---|---|
| "How have you found those problems to be solved since joining?" | "What's different about a typical week now compared with before? Walk me through one." |
| "When did temporary stop being temporary?" | "When you started, how long did you plan to stay? What happened after that?" |
| "So you feel more supported, less alone?" | "Tell me about the last time you got stuck on something. What did you do?" |
| "What would you want someone on the fence to know?" | "What did you worry about before you joined? What actually happened?" |
| "Finish the sentence: we're the only company that…" | Drop it. It produces messaging, not evidence. |

## Interviewer briefing

- Ask, then stop. Let silence sit; don't fill it.
- Don't summarize the person's story back before they've told it.
- Don't introduce words you hope they'll repeat (a feeling, a value, a brand phrase).
- Don't raise an objection or a problem before they do; ask what worried them.
- Ask for one specific example whenever an answer is general.
- If you manage or work with the person, say so on the record, and expect less candor about problems.
- For filmed interviews, keep the interviewer's voice off the moments you'll want to use, and let the person restate the question in their answer.
