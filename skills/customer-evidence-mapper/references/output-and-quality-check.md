# Output and Quality Check

## Required output

First produce the complete table:

| Persona | Type of Org | Use Case | Current Way | Limitation | Problem | Severity | New Way | Feature | Benefit | Wow Factor | Evidence / Quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then include these sections.

### 1. Strongest Validated Problems

Identify the 3–5 strongest chains. Summarize each as `Persona -> Problem -> Why it matters`. Prioritize evidence strength and severity rather than novelty.

### 2. Hypotheses That Still Need Validation

For each strategically interesting but unproven conclusion, distinguish:

- **Evidence says:** what was established.
- **Hypothesis:** what the team believes may follow.
- **Needs validation:** what evidence would increase confidence.

### 3. Evidence Gaps

Identify missing information, including an affected persona not interviewed directly, unclear severity or frequency, unclear workarounds, unknown willingness to change or pay, untested features, and downstream outcomes assumed rather than demonstrated.

### 4. Recommended Next Interviews

Recommend the next persona, the assumption to validate, the evidence that would change confidence, and 3–5 behavioral questions. Prioritize past behavior over hypothetical product preferences.

## What not to do

Do not summarize chronologically, assign every quote a row, assume the buyer or interviewee is the persona, treat secondhand claims as direct validation, treat feature requests as validated problems, manufacture severity, convert speculation to fact, over-index on future ideas, claim downstream ROI without evidence, use vague benefits such as “increase efficiency,” create unsupported marketing language, or add rows merely to make a proposed product appear valuable.

## Row-by-row audit

For every row, verify:

1. **Persona:** Is this the person who directly experiences the problem?
2. **Type of Org:** Does the context explain where or why it occurs?
3. **Use Case:** Is this a customer job rather than a product interaction?
4. **Current Way:** Did the interview establish how they accomplish it today?
5. **Limitation:** Is this specifically what is weak about the approach?
6. **Problem:** Is this the consequence rather than a restatement?
7. **Severity:** Is there actual evidence that it matters?
8. **New Way:** Is this a better behavior or workflow rather than premature implementation?
9. **Feature:** Does the proposed capability directly enable the New Way?
10. **Benefit:** Is this a believable first-order outcome?
11. **Wow Factor:** Is it compelling without strengthening the claim?
12. **Evidence:** Would the quote or paraphrase let a reader understand why the insight exists?

If any answer is no, rewrite, downgrade, flag, or remove the row.

## Standard of excellence

The finished analysis must clearly distinguish:

- what customers told us;
- what the evidence suggests;
- what remains hypothetical;
- what the team may choose to build.

The goal is not the largest table. It is the smallest set of high-severity, well-evidenced problems that can inform product strategy, research, messaging, and future decisions.
