# Evidence Integrity

## Certainty classification

Internally classify every insight:

- **Validated:** the interviewee directly described the workflow, problem, consequence, severity, or desired outcome.
- **Inferred:** the conclusion reasonably follows from evidence but was not explicitly stated.
- **Hypothesis:** the conclusion needs additional customer or market validation.

Do not add a classification column unless requested. Signal uncertainty inside relevant cells with “Hypothesis,” “Potentially,” “Emerging signal,” “Needs validation,” “Not yet validated,” “Inferred from…,” or “Interviewee believes…, but the affected persona has not been interviewed.”

## Quote integrity

Never invent a quote, rewrite a paraphrase as a quote, splice separate statements, strengthen wording, misattribute speakers, or remove context in a way that changes meaning. Attribute each participant correctly. Use quotation marks only for exact transcript language; identify paraphrases as paraphrases.

## Persona integrity

Ask who actually experiences the problem. Do not default to the buyer, interviewee, most senior participant, or assumed target customer.

Treat evidence about other people as secondhand. An executive describing employee work is weaker than interviewing the employee. An employee describing their own work is firsthand. An owner describing what customers supposedly want is not direct customer validation. Flag this distinction when it affects confidence.

## Problem integrity

Keep these separate:

- **Current Way:** what they do today.
- **Limitation:** what is structurally weak about it.
- **Problem:** the negative consequence that follows.

Do not produce three variations of “Information is fragmented.” A valid chain is: users gather information from several systems -> relevant context is fragmented -> users spend time reconstructing the situation before deciding.

## Feature discipline

Do not reverse-engineer customer pain from a favored solution. A feature request does not prove that the underlying problem is severe. Establish the job and pain, define the better workflow, then identify the smallest capability that might enable it.

## Benefit discipline

Trace causal distance:

`Feature -> Direct capability -> First-order benefit -> Second-order action -> Customer outcome -> Commercial outcome`

Example: automated signal -> user sees an important change -> user identifies situations sooner -> user acts sooner -> customer may get a better experience -> retention may improve -> revenue may increase.

Default to the first or second order. Do not present retention or revenue as established unless independently supported.

## Evidence strength

Increase confidence for repeated comments, strong emotional language, specific examples, quantified consequences, operational metrics, existing workarounds, explicit prioritization, frequent behavior, and clear cost or risk.

Reduce confidence when the interviewee speculates about others, describes aspirations rather than present pain, proposes features without establishing a problem, speaks for a persona not interviewed, mentions the problem only after prompting, or contradicts themselves.
