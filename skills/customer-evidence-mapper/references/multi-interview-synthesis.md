# Multi-Interview Synthesis

Do not concatenate findings interview by interview. Synthesize each problem across the evidence.

For each problem, examine:

- how many interviews mention it;
- which personas experience it;
- whether the same use case recurs;
- whether current workflows are similar;
- consistency of customer language;
- severity and frequency across interviews;
- existing workarounds;
- contradictory evidence;
- organizational characteristics associated with it.

Prioritize problems that recur, are severe when they occur, affect a strategically relevant group, are poorly served by current approaches, and have concrete behavioral evidence. If only one interview supports an insight, label it an emerging signal rather than a market-level conclusion.

## Contradictory evidence

Do not hide disagreement or force consensus. Contradiction can reveal segmentation. Investigate whether company size, maturity, role, industry, business model, workflow complexity, technology stack, customer type, or organizational structure explains the difference. Report meaningful contradictions explicitly.

## Customer language

Preserve vivid customer language when it is clearer than internal terminology. Prefer “People have to hunt across several systems before they know what is going on” over “Cross-functional information fragmentation creates workflow inefficiency.” The analysis can be sophisticated; the language should remain clear.
