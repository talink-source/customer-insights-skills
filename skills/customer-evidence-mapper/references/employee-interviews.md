# Employee Interviews

Use this mode when the interviewees are a company's own employees (or contractors, members, or partners) and the goal is employer value proposition (EVP), recruiting, retention or employer-brand evidence rather than product discovery. The causal chain and every integrity rule still apply. What changes is what each column means and which biases to watch for.

## How the columns change

| Column | Customer mode | Employee mode |
|---|---|---|
| Persona | The role that experiences the problem | The employee role whose working life is described (for example, frontline practitioner, team lead, new hire). Leaders and HQ staff describing other people's experiences are not the persona; see "Secondhand accounts" below. |
| Type of Org | The customer's environment | The employer setting the person came from or would otherwise work in (for example, a single-site employer, an agency, self-employment). |
| Use Case | The job the customer is trying to do | What the person is trying to achieve in their working life (for example, do good work and still have an evening, take real time off, keep a career through a move). |
| Current Way | How they do it today | How it worked before they joined, or how it works at the alternatives they weighed. |
| Limitation | Why the current way falls short | The structural reason the old setup fell short (for example, the employee owns every case continuously, so the work has no end point). |
| Problem | The negative consequence | The consequence for the person (lost evenings, guilt, burnout risk, stalled growth). |
| Severity | Frequency x impact x urgency | Same scale. Weight firsthand, specific, repeated accounts; note when people left a job over it. |
| New Way | The improved workflow | How it works for them now, in their own terms. |
| Feature | The product capability | The employer capability that makes the New Way possible, named in the company's own positioning or EVP terms when those exist. Mark capabilities the person credits to the job type in general rather than this employer as "Inferred." |
| Benefit | First-order benefit | What got better for the person. |
| Wow Factor | Customer-facing hook | A *draft* recruiting line. Mark it as draft; it is not approved copy. |
| Evidence / Quote | Exact quotes | Exact quotes with speaker and timecode. |

## Biases specific to employee interviews

Flag each of these in a "Sample caveats" section before the evidence map, and let them lower confidence where they apply.

- **Survivorship.** Interviewees are usually people who stayed. The evidence describes why people stay, not why prospects join or why others left. Say so.
- **The interviewer works for the employer.** Expect social desirability and leading questions. Treat any line that answers a leading or presupposing question as weaker evidence (see "Prompted" in [content-outputs.md](content-outputs.md)).
- **Secondhand accounts.** Leaders and HQ staff often describe what they hear from others. That is not direct validation of the employee's problem. Keep their firsthand stories (from when they did the frontline job) separate from their secondhand ones.
- **Company messaging.** Scripted prompts ("Finish the sentence," "Why us, in one sentence") produce messaging, not evidence. Don't use those answers as proof.
- **Credit to the job type vs. the employer.** People often credit benefits to the kind of work (for example, relief or remote work in general) rather than the specific employer. Separate the two in the Feature column.

## Checks before any line is used publicly

List these in a "Before public use" section:

- Confirm the speaker on video or audio; auto-transcripts mislabel speakers.
- Policy facts said on camera (benefits eligibility, leave, scheduling rules) need HR confirmation.
- Stories involving family members, grief, health or clients/patients need explicit consent.
- Mental-health lines need a safe-messaging review.
- Criticism of named employers, clients or partners stays out of external use.
- Check brand language the company avoids.

## Positioning check

When the company has a positioning document or EVP, score each of its elements against the interviews. See the positioning check format in [content-outputs.md](content-outputs.md). Report contradictions and missing evidence openly, and recommend changes rather than making them.
