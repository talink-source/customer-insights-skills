# Analysis Process

## 1. Read for jobs, not features

Identify what people try to accomplish, how they do it today, what frustrates them, what takes time or manual effort, what creates uncertainty, what they worry about, prioritize, or measure, what workarounds exist, and what they wish worked differently. Initially ignore proposed product ideas.

## 2. Identify problem chains

Group evidence into:

`Persona -> Use Case -> Current Way -> Limitation -> Problem -> Severity`

Do not assign every quote a row. Combine multiple quotes when they describe the same workflow and pain.

## 3. Separate personas

Determine whether evidence belongs to buyers, executives, managers, practitioners, administrators, end users, or customers. Create separate rows for meaningfully different problems, but do not force every possible persona into the output. Include only personas supported by meaningful evidence.

## 4. Evaluate evidence strength

Prioritize repeated comments, emotion, specific examples, quantified consequences, operational metrics, workarounds, explicit priority, frequent behavior, and clear cost or risk. Downgrade speculation, aspiration, prompted claims, secondhand accounts, feature requests without an underlying job, and contradictions.

## 5. Design the New Way

For each meaningful problem, ask: if this were improved, what would the person be able to do differently? Describe that behavior before thinking about implementation.

## 6. Map potential features

Identify the smallest plausible capability needed for the New Way. Prefer a narrow capability that solves an important problem over a broad platform. Do not assume customers need a comprehensive product because they mentioned several related needs.

## 7. Identify the first-order benefit

Ask what immediately becomes easier, faster, clearer, safer, or more reliable. Keep the benefit close to the capability.

## 8. Express the Wow Factor

Translate the benefit into concise, compelling customer language without increasing the claim’s strength. Clarity matters more than cleverness.

## Recommended interview questions

Prioritize past behavior over hypothetical preferences:

- Tell me about the last time this happened.
- Walk me through exactly what you did.
- What happened next?
- How did you know there was a problem?
- Where did you look for information?
- Who else was involved?
- How long did it take?
- How often does this happen?
- What happens if you do not solve it?
- What have you tried instead?
- What is the hardest part?
- When does this become urgent?

Avoid “Would you use this feature?”, “Would this dashboard be useful?”, “Would AI solve this?”, “Would automation save time?”, or “Would you pay for this?” during problem discovery. Hypothetical questions may be used later for concept testing but cannot replace behavioral evidence.
